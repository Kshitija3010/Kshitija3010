# आकाश का नक्शा · The Map of the Sky
Style: Silkscreen Travel Poster (full-bleed 16:9 prints) · Topic: the mysterious world of astrology · ~120 s · Hindi voice + Hindi printed subtitles · 1920×1080, 24 fps, −14 LUFS.

## Brief (defaults filled in)
- Voice: Kokoro `hf_alpha` (Hindi, offline). Microsoft edge-tts is unreachable from this machine; Whisper model download is blocked, so the automatic ASR check cannot run (phonemes checked instead).
- No user material; no storyboard stop.
- Tone: curious and respectful, honest about the science. Astrology is shown as one of humanity's oldest stories about the sky, not as proven prediction.

## 1. Three candidate structures
1. **Twelve signs, twelve posters** (destination series): one print per rashi, then a wall. Easy, but it is a list with no turn, and twelve honest reading holds already fill 2 minutes.
2. **One sky, re-inked through the night**: a single rooftop poster re-inked hour by hour while a family reads a kundli. Intimate, but it can't carry history or the precession reveal.
3. **A ticket through time** (chosen): a traveller's ticket stub counts the years as the film travels from poster to poster, place to place, along the path humans took reading the sky: Babylon → Alexandria → Jaipur → a 1930 newspaper city → the turn: *the sky itself has moved*. Ending: what the search gave us.

**Why 3:** it has a subject (the human question "do the stars write our fate?"), a journey, and a real turn that only this medium can show: the *same* sky poster re-inked 2,000 years later with the signs slipped out of register. Precession is literally a registration slip.

## 2. Logline and arc
For 2,500 years people have read the sky as a map of fate; a ticket stub follows that map from Babylon to today, until we discover the map itself has drifted, and that the real gift of looking up was the questions.
- **Setup (0–12 s):** a blank night is printed; stars are paper pinholes. Question asked.
- **Journey (12–80 s):** Babylon, Alexandria, Jaipur, the 1930 newspaper.
- **Turn (80–100 s):** silence; the zodiac ring slips out of register by 24°. Earth's wobble.
- **Ending (100–120 s):** science's verdict and the gift: calendars, maths, astronomy. Series wall at night, the question answered with a softer one.

## 3. Benchmark
- **WPA-era national-park screen prints / mid-century airline posters**: learn layer economy, stepped skies, dark info band. Don't take compositions, lettering, logos.
- **Saul Bass-era title sequences** (for rhythm only): one graphic idea per beat, the hit on the downbeat. Don't take any shapes.

## 5. Beat sheet / cue map
Tempo 84 bpm (beat 0.714 s, bar 2.857 s). Instruments: tanpura-style drone (synth), plucked sitar-like line (pluck), frame drum + hand drum strokes for pulls, hand chimes for stars, harmonium-like reed pad (accordion samples, quiet). Every pull starts on a beat; info items land on eighths.
- 0–12 intro: drone + chimes on each star punch. Title on bar 3 downbeat.
- 12–80 journey: plucked melody enters, frame drum on pulls; each poster change = a drum hit + whoosh of the silhouette.
- 80–86 **silence 1**: drone only, then music out entirely; first sound after = loud registration clack.
- 86–100 turn: sparse pulse, low drum on "24°".
- 100–104 **silence 2** before last line; then full melody returns on the wall dolly; final chord at 116.

## 7. Subtitles and titles
- Voice lines are printed as a dark **subtitle strip** at the foot of the print (a band pull), Hindi in a condensed Devanagari display (Teko), cream ink on near-dark.
- Headers printed into the sky in Teko, tracked; info band labels in Poppins (Devanagari).
- Ticket stub (top-right): "यात्रा" + era year, re-stamped per poster.

## 8. Facts (see FACTS.md)
Oldest known horoscope ~410 BCE (Babylon); 12 equal zodiac signs from Babylonian astronomy (~5th c. BCE); Ptolemy's Tetrabiblos (2nd c. CE, Alexandria); 27 nakshatras, 12 rashis, 9 grahas; Jantar Mantar Jaipur, completed 1734 by Sawai Jai Singh II; first newspaper star column 1930 (London); precession ~1° per 72 years, cycle ~26,000 years, sidereal–tropical offset ~24° today; controlled tests (e.g. Carlson, Nature 1985) found astrologers did no better than chance.
