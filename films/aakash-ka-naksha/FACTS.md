# FACTS · आकाश का नक्शा
Every factual claim in the narration and on the prints, with a source. Wording is paraphrased in Hindi in the film.

| Claim in the film | Source |
|---|---|
| Babylonian priests/scribes recorded planetary movements and omens on clay tablets (~2,500 years ago and earlier) | Enūma Anu Enlil omen series and the Babylonian Astronomical Diaries (from 652 BCE). Britannica, "Astrology: Mesopotamia" |
| The zodiac of 12 equal signs comes from Babylonian astronomy | Developed by Babylonian astronomers around the 5th century BCE. Britannica, "Zodiac" |
| The oldest known horoscope dates to 410 BCE | Babylonian cuneiform horoscope, 29 April 410 BCE (Sachs, "Babylonian Horoscopes", J. Cuneiform Studies 6, 1952) |
| In Alexandria, Ptolemy wrote down the rules of planets and signs in a book (2nd century) | Claudius Ptolemy, *Tetrabiblos*, Alexandria, 2nd century CE |
| Jyotisha: the Moon's path divided into 27 nakshatras; 9 grahas; 12 rashis | Standard Jyotisha (Vedanga Jyotisha for nakshatras; navagraha). Britannica, "Hindu astrology / nakshatra" |
| 18th century: Raja (Sawai) Jai Singh II built a giant stone observatory in Jaipur; its sundial still tells the time; seal "1734" | Jantar Mantar, Jaipur, completed c. 1734; the Samrat Yantra is a giant sundial. UNESCO World Heritage listing 1338 |
| 1930: a London newspaper printed a column about the stars; daily horoscopes followed | R. H. Naylor's horoscope article in the *Sunday Express*, August 1930, which led to the regular star column |
| Earth wobbles like a spinning top; the stars shift about 1° every 72 years | Axial precession ≈ 50.3″ per year ≈ 1° per 71.6 years; full cycle ≈ 25,800 years |
| Today the Sun is about 24° ahead of the old signs; this is why Indian (sidereal) and Western (tropical) signs differ | Lahiri ayanamsa ≈ 24.2° in 2026 (offset between sidereal and tropical zodiacs) |
| Scientific tests have not found astrology's predictions better than chance | Carlson, S. "A double-blind test of astrology", *Nature* 318, 419–425 (1985); later reviews agree |
| Watching the sky gave us calendars, mathematics and astronomy | General history of astronomy (Babylonian mathematical astronomy, calendrical astronomy in India) |
