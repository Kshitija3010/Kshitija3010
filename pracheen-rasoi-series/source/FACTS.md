# Facts and sources · Ep 1 · दुनिया की पहली रोटी

| Claim in the film | Source |
|---|---|
| Charred bread remains found at Shubayqa 1, a Natufian hunter-gatherer site in the Black Desert (Harrat al-Sham), northeastern Jordan | Arranz-Otaegui, A. et al. (2018), "Archaeobotanical evidence reveals the origins of bread 14,400 years ago in northeastern Jordan", *PNAS* 115(31): 7925–7930 ([PMC6077754](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6077754)) |
| About 14,400 years old (radiocarbon, give or take a few centuries) | Same paper; University of Copenhagen / UCL press coverage, July 2018 |
| The Natufians were hunter-gatherers, not farmers | Same paper (Natufian culture, Late Epipalaeolithic) |
| Found in a stone-built fireplace inside a structure | Same paper: remains came from fireplaces in a basalt-built structure |
| Ingredients: wild einkorn (a wild relative of wheat) and club-rush tubers (a sedge that grows near water) | Same paper: wild cereals such as einkorn and club-rush (*Bolboschoenus glaucus*) tubers |
| Grind → knead → bake; unleavened flatbread | Same paper: microscopic structure shows grinding, sieving, kneading and baking; low porosity, so unleavened flatbread-like products |
| Baked on hot stones or in the ashes of the fireplace | Same paper's discussion of likely baking methods (stated in the film as the likely way, "या राख में") |
| 24 charred food remains analysed with microscopes, 2018 | Same paper (scanning electron microscopy of 24 remains) |
| Bread came at least 4,000 years before farming | Same paper: "at least 4,000 years prior to the emergence of agriculture" |
| "Perhaps bread is why people later started to grow cereals" (said as शायद, a hypothesis) | The authors suggest bread production may have encouraged plant cultivation (Arranz-Otaegui et al. 2018; press interviews) |

Timeline on the page: bread at 14,400 years ago; "the start of farming" placed 4,000 years later, as "at least 4,000 years". No exact farming date is claimed.
